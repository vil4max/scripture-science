## Judaism (Иудаизм)

*Covers Orthodox (Haredi and Modern Orthodox), Conservative/Masorti and Reform/Progressive streams together, noting differences inline. Karaites and Samaritans are treated as separate brief entries below.*

### 1. Identity and self-understanding

**Founding event.** Judaism understands itself as founded on God's covenant with Abraham and, decisively, on the revelation of the Torah to Moses at Mount Sinai.

- The Orthodox Union states the Torah "was given to them by G-d at Mt. Sinai, some thirty three hundred years ago," a belief it calls *Torah min HaShamayim* ("Torah comes from Heaven").
  **[official]** [OU, "Torah"](https://www.ou.org/judaism-101/glossary/torah/) — "given to them by G-d at Mt. Sinai, some thirty three hundred years ago."

**The Jewish calendar encodes this self-understanding.** Years are counted *Anno Mundi* ("from the creation of the world"); epoch year 1 begins with a new moon "6 октября 3761 г. до н.э. по юлианскому календарю," per the rabbinic chronology *Seder Olam Rabbah*.

- **[reference]** [Astronet, "Еврейский календарь"](https://www.astronet.ru/db/msg/1182321/node5.html) — "Новолуние, с которого начался год AM 1... 6 октября 3761 г. до н.э. по юлианскому календарю."

**Traditional vs. scholarly dating:**

| | Traditional | Scholarly |
|---|---|---|
| Abraham | Placed in the patriarchal era, 2nd millennium BCE (no confirmed AM figure this session, see TODO) | "it seems impossible to determine the period in which he lived" — **[reference]** [My Jewish Learning, "Abraham"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/abraham/) |
| Exodus / Sinai | Roughly "thirty-three hundred years" before a ca.-1998 OU article (i.e. ~14th–13th c. BCE) — **[official]** OU, above | Split between two windows: "most people settle into two camps: They argue for either a 15th-century B.C.E. or 13th-century B.C.E. date"; evidence "generally supports a 13th-century B.C.E. Exodus during the Ramesside Period" — **[reference]** [Biblical Archaeology Society, "Exodus: Fact or Fiction?"](https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/biblical-topics/exodus/exodus-fact-or-fiction/) |

The Biblical Archaeology Society source also cautions that archaeology "can't be used to 'prove' that every detail of the Exodus story in the Bible is true."

### 3. Authority and doctrinal sources

The Written Torah is read together with the **Oral Torah**, codified in the **Talmud**.

- "Талмуд является... фундаментальный труд еврейских мудрецов, являющийся упорядоченной записью Устной Торы," которая "была дана еврейскому народу в устной форме и передавалась устно из поколения в поколение."
  **[official]** [Toldot.com, "Талмуд — Устная Тора"](https://toldot.com/talmud.html).
- The Oral Torah, like the Written Torah: "They received it at Sinai, along with the Written Torah."
  **[official]** [OU, "Torah"](https://www.ou.org/judaism-101/glossary/torah/).

**Who interprets — the streams diverge sharply here:**

- **Orthodox**: halakha is normative; its development proceeds only through the traditional rabbinic process. Orthodox Judaism is "more resistant than its liberal counterparts to the idea that biblical mandates can change in response to changing social circumstances."
  **[reference]** [My Jewish Learning, "Orthodox Judaism"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/orthodox-judaism/) — "more resistant than its liberal counterparts to the idea that biblical mandates can change."
- **Conservative/Masorti**: halakha is binding but "subject to historical development"; central legal body is the Committee on Jewish Law and Standards. The movement combines "fidelity to inherited tradition and the courage to integrate necessary change." "Each rabbi serves as halakhic authority for his or her community, and our rabbis collectively give direction."
  **[official]** [Rabbinical Assembly, "Conservative/Masorti"](https://www.rabbinicalassembly.org/about-us/conservative-masorti).
  Summary: "maintaining (unlike Reform) that Jewish law remains binding on modern Jews, but affording far greater leeway than Orthodoxy in adapting those laws."
  **[reference]** [My Jewish Learning, "Conservative Judaism"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/conservative-judaism/).
- **Reform/Progressive**: individual conscience is the final authority. "Reform Jews, as members of a group and as individuals... strive to make thoughtful choices about how we put our values into action," within "the Covenant between God and Israel as expressed... in the teachings of an ever-evolving Torah and tradition."
  **[official]** [URJ, "What is Reform Judaism?"](https://urj.org/what-we-believe/what-is-reform-judaism).

### 4. Adherents

**Global figure.** 15,736,800 core Jews worldwide as of 1 January 2024.

- "On January 1, 2024, we estimated the world's Jewish population at 15,736,800, an actual increase of 78,800 (0.50%) above the revised 2023 estimate of 15,658,000."
  **[reference]** DellaPergola, "World Jewish Population, 2024," in *American Jewish Year Book 2024* (Springer, 2025), [hosted by Israel CBS (PDF)](https://www.cbs.gov.il/he/Documents/World%20DellaPergola%202024%20complete.pdf).
- Largest communities (same source, same page): Israel 7,153,000; United States 6,300,000; France 438,500; Canada 400,000; UK 313,000; Argentina 170,000; Germany 125,000; Russian Federation 123,000; Australia 117,000.
- **Counting method**: the "core Jewish population" concept, defined as "a human collective whose identification is mutually exclusive with respect to other subpopulations" — i.e. excludes people of partial/mixed Jewish ancestry who do not themselves identify as Jewish. Same source.

**United States** (largest single diaspora community): "the 2020 Jewish population estimate is approximately 7.5 million, including 5.8 million adults and 1.8 million children (rounded to the closest 100,000)."

- **[reference]** [Pew Research Center, "Jewish Americans in 2020"](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2021/05/11/jewish-americans-in-2020/) (PDF opened and text-extracted in this session) — "approximately 7.5 million, including 5.8 million adults and 1.8 million children."
- Counting method: combines "Jews by religion" and "Jews of no religion" (people with a Jewish parent/upbringing who identify culturally/ethnically as Jewish but profess no religion): "Together, these two groups comprise the net Jewish population."

**US denominational breakdown** (Pew, same report, 2020 survey data):

- "Reform (37%) or Conservative (17%)... about one-in-ten (9%) identify with Orthodox Judaism. One-third of Jews (32%) do not identify with any particular Jewish denomination, and 4% identify with smaller branches."
  **[reference]** same Pew source as above.

### 5. Positions on the comparison topics

**God (oneness, nature).**

- Maimonides' (Rambam's) Thirteen Principles of Faith (*13 принципов веры*), verified verbatim via direct raw-HTML fetch, principles 2–4:
  - Unity: "Безоговорочно верю в то, что Творец, благословенно имя Его, — Один, и нет в мире цельности, подобной абсолютной цельности Его."
  - Incorporeality: "Творец... нематериален и ему несвойственны присущие материи состояния, и что у Него вообще нет никакого подобия."
  - Eternity: "Творец... — начало всего и конец всего."
  **[official]** [Toldot.com, "13 принципов веры"](https://toldot.com/13PrintsipovVery.html).
- Proclaimed daily in the *Shema* (Deuteronomy 6:4): "Слушай, Израиль: Господь – Бог наш, Господь один!"
  **[official]** [ru.Chabad.org, "Шма Исраэль"](https://ru.chabad.org/library/article_cdo/aid/2655201) — "Слушай, Израиль: Господь – Бог наш, Господь один!"
- All three mainstream streams affirm strict monotheism. `TODO: verify` stream-specific differences in reading divine incorporeality/attributes philosophically — no primary source found per-stream in this session.

**The name of God.**

- The Tetragrammaton (יהוה) is written but not pronounced as spelled: "Пишется оно Йуд-Кей-Вав-Кей, а читается Адой-ной." "Из уважения к этому Имени мы не произносим его так, как оно написано... мы произносим Адой-ной (Господь)."
  **[official]** [Toldot.com, "Как зовут Б-га?"](https://toldot.com/urava/ask/urava_5809.html).
- Outside prayer, reverent substitutes are used: "а-Шем" (literally "the Name") is referenced explicitly on the same page; "Всевышний" ("the Most High") is used pervasively as the Russian substitute term across Toldot.com and Chabad.org material (e.g. in the 13-principles text: "Творец, благословенно имя Его").

**Jesus, the prophets and the Messiah.**

- Moses is unsurpassed among the prophets — principle 7: "пророчество Моше-Рабейну... истина и что он — величайший из всех пророков, бывших до него и пришедших после него."
  **[official]** Toldot.com, 13 principles (above).
- The Messiah is still awaited — principle 12: "Безоговорочно верю в приход Машиаха, и, хотя он задерживается, я всё же каждый день буду ждать его."
  **[official]** same source.
- On Jesus specifically: "Authoritative texts of Judaism reject Jesus as God, Divine Being, intermediary between humans and God, Messiah or saint." Christian claims "that Jesus is the textual messiah of the Hebrew Bible" are seen "as being based on mistranslations, given that Jesus did not fulfill any of the Jewish Messiah qualifications."
  **[reference]** [My Jewish Learning, "What Do Jews Believe About Jesus?"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/what-do-jews-believe-about-jesus/).
- The same article: Christian claims that Jesus is "equal to God and as divine" are "a belief hard to reconcile with Judaism's insistence on God's oneness."
- Reform Judaism's 1885 Pittsburgh Platform historically "formally renounced... the doctrines of bodily resurrection" and reframed messianism (see "afterlife" below). `TODO: verify` further stream-specific nuance on Jesus/Messiah beyond this.

**The spirit of God.**

- Hebrew *ruach Elohim* (Genesis 1:2): "וְר֣וּחַ אֱלֹהִ֔ים מְרַחֶ֖פֶת עַל־פְּנֵ֥י הַמָּֽיִם" — Sefaria's (JPS-derived) English renders this "a wind from... God," with an explicit footnote: "In contrast to others 'the spirit of.'"
  **[reference]** Sefaria API, `GET /api/texts/Genesis.1.2` (opened via `curl`).
- Judaism does not develop a distinct hypostasized "Holy Spirit" person comparable to Christian pneumatology. `TODO: verify` — no dedicated primary/reference source on *ruach ha-kodesh* as a developed doctrinal category was successfully opened in this session (a My Jewish Learning URL guess 404'd).

**Scripture and canon.** See §2 — all streams accept the same 24-book Tanakh as canonical; they differ on the Oral Torah's status (§3) and on how literally the Written Torah's origin narrative is read (§1).

**Tradition/Oral Law, and who interprets.** See §3 for the three streams' differing authorities (traditional rabbinic process / CJLS committee / individual autonomy).

**Salvation/redemption.**

- Reward and punishment for observance of the commandments — principle 11: "Творец... отвечает добром соблюдающим Его заповеди и карает тех, кто их нарушает."
  **[official]** Toldot.com, 13 principles (above).
- Judaism teaches covenantal life lived according to Torah/halakha (Orthodox/Conservative) or its ethical core as autonomously appropriated (Reform), not salvation through creedal belief alone — per the authority differences in §3.

**Soul, death, afterlife and judgement.**

- Resurrection of the dead — principle 13: "Безоговорочно верю в то, что мёртвые вернутся к жизни."
  **[official]** Toldot.com, 13 principles (above).
- The rabbinic doctrine of *Olam Ha-Ba* ("грядущий мир," the World to Come): "Кардинальная эсхатологическая доктрина олам ха-ба в иудаизме состоит в вере в воскресение из мертвых." The Talmud: "Тот, кто говорит, что воскресение мертвых не подкрепляется Торой, не получит доли в олам ха-ба."
  **[reference]** [Электронная еврейская энциклопедия (ОРТ), "Олам ха-ба"](https://eleven.co.il/judaism/theology/13061/).
- Historically, Reform Judaism's 1885 Pittsburgh Platform broke with this doctrine: it "formally renounced... the doctrines of bodily resurrection and heaven and hell."
  **[reference]** [My Jewish Learning, "Reform Judaism"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/reform-judaism/) — "formally renounced... the doctrines of bodily resurrection and heaven and hell."
- `TODO: verify` the current (post-1885, e.g. 1999 "Pittsburgh Principles"-era) official URJ position on afterlife/resurrection specifically — not confirmed against a primary source opened this session.

**End times and the future of the world.**

- Tied to the Messiah (principle 12) and *Olam Ha-Ba*; classical sources debate whether the messianic age is a prelude to, or separate from, the World to Come: "одни учителя считали, что золотой век Мессии — это только преддверие олам ха-ба, другие полагали, что новый порядок наступит «только после окончания мессианской эры»."
  **[reference]** Электронная еврейская энциклопедия, "Олам ха-ба" (above).

**Worship and rites (prayer, commandments).**

- Tradition counts 613 commandments (*mitzvot*) in the Torah; roughly fifty concern food/kashrut alone: "Из 613 заповедей, данных нашему народу Творцом, около пятидесяти посвящены вопросам питания."
  **[official]** [Toldot.com, "Кашрут — кошерное и некошерное"](https://toldot.com/kashrut.html).
- Prayer is addressed to God alone — principle 5: "одному лишь Творцу... следует молиться, — Ему, и никому другому."
  **[official]** Toldot.com, 13 principles (above).

**Images.**

- Torah prohibition, Exodus 20:4 (Second Commandment): "You shall not make for yourself a sculptured image, or any likeness of what is in the heavens above, or on the earth below, or in the waters under the earth."
  **[reference]** Sefaria API, `GET /api/texts/Exodus.20.4` (opened via `curl`).
- This underlies both the incorporeality doctrine (principle 3 above) and Judaism's general historical avoidance of figurative religious imagery. `TODO: verify` stream-specific differences in applying this today (e.g. figurative synagogue art in non-Orthodox contexts) — not sourced in this session.

**Holy days, sabbath, festivals.** `TODO: verify` — a dedicated primary source enumerating the festival calendar was not opened in this session (outside the specific brief questions this session prioritized); the Shema and Sabbath/festival cycle are structured by the Torah/Oral Torah framework documented in §2–3.

**Organisation and clergy (rabbis).**

- Ordination (*semikha*, "from the Hebrew word *l'smoch*, meaning 'to lean on'") historically involved the laying of hands; "Today, the practice of rabbinic ordination does not include any 'laying of the hands,' but a written document."
  **[reference]** [Jewish Virtual Library, "Rabbinic Ordination (Semikha)"](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/rabbinic-ordination-semikha).
- Orthodox rabbis are ordained by "Orthodox *yeshivot*"; Conservative and Reform candidates are ordained by their own movements' seminaries (same source).
- "The Reconstructionist, Reform, and Conservative movements all ordain women for the Rabbinate," while "Most of mainstream Orthodoxy does not share her sentiments" (referring to the first Orthodox-affiliated female ordination, 2009). Same source.

**Distinctive rules — kashrut.**

- "Тора подробно разъясняет какие виды животных разрешены в пищу, а какие запрещены" — kosher land animals must chew the cud and have split hooves; meat and dairy are kept separate; blood is prohibited.
  **[official]** [Toldot.com, "Кашрут"](https://toldot.com/kashrut.html).
- Observance of kashrut and other ritual law is where the three streams differ most visibly in practice, tracking the authority differences in §3.

### 6. Sensitive points and common misconceptions to avoid

- Judaism is not a single monolithic "denomination": Orthodox (further split into Haredi and Modern Orthodox), Conservative/Masorti and Reform/Progressive differ substantially on halakhic authority (§3) while sharing the same canonical Tanakh (§2).
- "Orthodox" as a label only came into use "in response to the introduction of Reform Judaism" in the 19th century — it is not itself an ancient self-designation, though Orthodox Jews understand their practice as continuous with Sinai.
  **[reference]** [My Jewish Learning, "Orthodox Judaism"](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/orthodox-judaism/) — "the term came into common use only in response to the introduction of Reform Judaism."
- Do not describe Jewish non-acceptance of Jesus as "rejection" or hostility; frame it as theological incompatibility ("a belief hard to reconcile with Judaism's insistence on God's oneness"), not condemnation of Jesus as a historical Jewish teacher.
- "Messianic Jews" (Jews for Jesus and similar groups) are a distinct phenomenon from normative Judaism; mainstream Jewish sources do not count them as a stream of Judaism. `TODO: verify` — an explicit Jewish-institutional (not just journalistic) statement to this effect was not opened in this session.
- Avoid writing out the Tetragrammaton or treating "Jehovah"/"Yahweh" as a confirmed pronunciation; Jewish sources treat the true pronunciation as lost / not to be reconstructed (§5, "the name of God").
- Present kashrut in the spiritual/covenantal terms Jewish sources use, not primarily as a health regulation — see the Toldot.com framing: "Кашрут — лекарство от духовной невосприимчивости" ("kashrut, a remedy for spiritual insensitivity").

## Karaite Judaism (Караимство), brief

### 1. Identity/self-understanding

Karaites ("Follower[s] of Scripture," from Hebrew *Karaim*/*Bnei Mikra*) hold that "all of the commandments handed down by Moses were recorded in the written Torah, and that an Oral Law was not given at Mount Sinai," rejecting "the Mishnah, Talmud, or Rabbinic decrees as binding."

- **[official]** Karaite Jews of America content, quoted via [Jewish Virtual Library, "Karaites"](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/karaites) — "Karaites maintain that all of the divine commandments handed down to Moses by God were recorded in the written Torah without additional Oral Law."

**Continuity, in the movement's own words**: Anan ben David (8th century) "was a political leader who convinced the Muslim authorities not to allow the Rabbanites to continue persecuting those Jews who believed in the exclusive authority of the Tanach" — i.e. Karaites present Anan as enabling, not founding, their tradition.

- **[official]** [Karaite-korner.org FAQ](https://www.karaite-korner.org/karaite_faq.shtml) — "Anan ben David was a political leader who convinced the Muslim authorities not to allow the Rabbanites to continue persecuting those Jews who believed in the exclusive authority of the Tanach."

Independently, an academic reference source dates organized Karaism's emergence to "the 9th century C.E., a number of sects arose that denied the existence of oral Torah."

- **[reference]** [Jewish Virtual Library, "Karaites"](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/karaites).

### 2–3. Scripture and authority

Only the Tanakh is authoritative: "Do Karaites accept the Oral Law/Talmud? No."

- **[official]** [karaite-korner.org FAQ](https://www.karaite-korner.org/karaite_faq.shtml).

No rabbinic interpretive monopoly: it is "the personal responsibility of every individual Jew to study the Torah, and ultimately decide for him or herself its correct meaning."

- **[reference]**/**[official]** summarized from Jewish Virtual Library and karaite-korner.org, both opened directly in this session.

### 4. Adherents

Historically "this movement at one time attracted as much as 40 percent of the Jewish people"; today "Karaites are a very small minority," with communities noted in Egypt (until 1967) and Los Angeles.

- **[reference]** [Jewish Virtual Library, "Karaites"](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/karaites) — "this movement at one time attracted as much as 40 percent of the Jewish people."

### 5. Distinctive positions (vs. Rabbinic Judaism)

Strict textualism, e.g. on the Sabbath: "the Karaites noted that the Bible specifically prohibits lighting a flame on the Sabbath, so they kept their houses dark," where Rabbanite practice "relied upon rabbinical interpretation that allowed [leaving] burning a flame."

- **[reference]** [Jewish Virtual Library, "Karaites"](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/karaites).

### 6. Sensitive points

Karaites consider themselves fully Jewish and reject being characterized as a Rabbinic-Judaism "offshoot" or heresy. Per their own FAQ, they deny that Anan ben David "founded" their religion out of nothing (§1 above) — present this as their self-understanding, alongside the Jewish Virtual Library's independent 9th-century dating of the organized movement, without adjudicating between them.

## Samaritanism (Самаритянство), brief

### 1. Identity/self-understanding

Samaritans call themselves "шамрим" ("хранители," keepers), in full "шамрим ал ха-эмет" — "хранители истины" (keepers of the truth). They consolidated as a religio-ethnic group "в начале эпохи Второго храма на территории Самарии" (at the start of the Second Temple period, in Samaria).

- **[reference]** [Электронная еврейская энциклопедия, "Самаритяне"](https://eleven.co.il/judaism-trends/samaritans/) — "Самаритяне консолидировались в качестве религиозно-этнической группы в начале эпохи Второго храма."

### 2. Scripture

Samaritans use only their own version of the Pentateuch: "древнееврейский текст Пятикнижия с некоторыми изменениями," written in Paleo-Hebrew script (the Samaritan Pentateuch); they do not accept the rest of the Tanakh.

- **[reference]** same source as above.

### 3. Authority

Centered on Mount Gerizim, "самое священное место в Эрец-Исраэль" for Samaritans, near Shechem/Nablus, where the community built its religious center "в период 8–5 веков до н. э."

- **[reference]** same source as above.

### 4. Adherents

"Религиозно-этническая группа, численностью около 600 человек, проживающая в отдельном квартале Холона и в Шхеме."

- **[reference]** [Электронная еврейская энциклопедия, "Самаритяне"](https://eleven.co.il/judaism-trends/samaritans/) — "численностью около 600 человек."

### 5. Positions

Strict monotheism and exclusive reliance on their own Pentateuch (§2). Moses as sole/supreme prophet and Mount Gerizim (not Jerusalem) as the chosen sanctuary site are widely reported claims about Samaritan belief, but `TODO: verify` — these two specific points were **not** confirmed against a primary or reference page opened directly in this session (only encountered in WebSearch summaries), so they are flagged rather than asserted as sourced fact here.

### 6. Sensitive points

Samaritans are religiously and ethnically distinct from Jews, not a Jewish "sect" or offshoot in their own self-understanding, though closely related historically. Avoid conflating them with unrelated groups or treating "Samaritan" as a purely biblical-historical category — the community is living, ~600 people today per the figure above.
