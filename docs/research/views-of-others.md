# Views of Other Traditions, in Each Tradition's Own Words

Companion to `orthodox-view-of-separated-groups.md`, which covers the Orthodox
side (St Basil's Canon 1; the 2000 «Основные принципы...»; the 1906 draft). This
file does the same for Catholic, Protestant, Adventist, Latter-day Saint,
Jehovah's Witness, Jewish and Islamic sources: how each tradition classifies or
describes the others, from that tradition's own official or representative
texts, opened directly in this session.

**Sourcing rules applied:** every quote below is followed by the URL of a page
opened directly in this session (via `curl`/WebFetch), with the exact verbatim
excerpt (≤25 words, original language) that supports it. `TODO: verify` marks
anything not pinned to a directly-opened source.

**Source tiers:** **[official]** — the tradition's own governing body, council
or canonical text. **[reference]** — neutral academic/statistical source.
**[news/NGO]** — reputable media, used only where no official source exists.

**Status matters as much as content.** A conciliar decree, a doctrinal note
issued by a standing office, and a statement signed by individual scholars
carry different institutional weight; each entry below says which it is.

Order follows the task brief (Catholic → Protestant → 19th-century
Restorationist/Adventist movements → Judaism → Islam), not the site's
eventual age-ordering rule (`EDITORIAL.md` §2), since this is a research
dossier, not page copy.

## Catholic Church

### Unitatis Redintegratio (Decree on Ecumenism)
- **Issuing body:** the Second Vatican Council, promulgated by Pope Paul VI. **Date:** 21 November 1964. **Status:** conciliar decree (binding magisterial text).
- Source opened: https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decree_19641121_unitatis-redintegratio_en.html — [official]
- On divisions in general: "quite large communities came to be separated from full communion with the Catholic Church - for which, often enough, men of both sides were to blame" (§3).
- On the Eastern (Orthodox) churches specifically: "These Churches, although separated from us, possess true sacraments, above all by apostolic succession, the priesthood and the Eucharist" (§15).
- Distinguishing the two kinds of division: "divisions arose more than four centuries later in the West, stemming from the events which are usually referred to as 'The Reformation'" (§13).
- On Reformation-era communities: "we believe they have not retained the proper reality of the eucharistic mystery in its fullness" (§22), because of "the absence of the sacrament of Orders."
- On all baptized Christians outside Rome: "all who have been justified by faith in Baptism are members of Christ's body... and so are correctly accepted as brothers" (§3).

### Nostra Aetate (Declaration on the Relation of the Church to Non-Christian Religions)
- **Issuing body:** the Second Vatican Council, proclaimed by Pope Paul VI. **Date:** 28 October 1965. **Status:** conciliar declaration.
- Source opened: https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html — [official]
- On Muslims (§3): "They adore the one God, living and subsisting in Himself; merciful and all-powerful, the Creator of heaven and earth."
- Also: "Though they do not acknowledge Jesus as God, they revere Him as a prophet... they also honor Mary, His virgin Mother."
- On Jews (§4): "the Jews should not be presented as rejected or accursed by God, as if this followed from the Holy Scriptures."
- Also: the Church "decries hatred, persecutions, displays of anti-Semitism, directed against Jews at any time and by anyone."
- On Hindus and Buddhists (§2, general characterization, not a doctrinal ruling): "Buddhism, in its various forms, realizes the radical insufficiency of this changeable world."

### Dominus Iesus (Declaration on the Unicity and Salvific Universality of Jesus Christ and the Church)
- **Issuing body:** Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, approved by Pope John Paul II. **Date:** 6 August 2000. **Status:** doctrinal declaration.
- Source opened: https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000806_dominus-iesus_en.html — [official]
- On other religions' scriptures (§8): the Church's tradition "reserves the designation of inspired texts to the canonical books of the Old and New Testaments."
- On Eastern (Orthodox) churches (§17): they "are true particular Churches" though lacking "full communion with the Catholic Church."
- On Reformation-era communities (§17): they "are not Churches in the proper sense; however, those who are baptized in these communities are... incorporated in Christ."
- On followers of other religions generally (§22): "objectively speaking they are in a gravely deficient situation in comparison with those who, in the Church, have the fullness of the means of salvation."

### Responses to Some Questions Regarding Certain Aspects of the Doctrine on the Church
- **Issuing body:** Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, ratified by Pope Benedict XVI. **Date:** 29 June 2007. **Status:** doctrinal responsa (five-question note).
- Source opened: https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20070629_responsa-quaestiones_en.html — [official]
- On why Orthodox/Eastern bodies are called "Churches" (Q4): "these Churches, although separated, have true sacraments and above all – because of the apostolic succession – the priesthood and the Eucharist."
- On why 16th-century Reformation communities are not (Q5): "these Communities do not enjoy apostolic succession in the sacrament of Orders, and are, therefore, deprived of a constitutive element of the Church."
- Explicit conclusion (Q5): such communities "cannot, according to Catholic doctrine, be called 'Churches' in the proper sense."

## Protestant Families

### Smalcald Articles, Part II, Article IV, "Of the Papacy" (Lutheran)
- **Issuing body:** written by Martin Luther for the 1537 Smalcald convention of the Lutheran princes; canonized as a confessional standard in the Book of Concord (1580). **Date:** 1537 / 1580. **Status:** binding subordinate confessional document for confessional Lutheran churches.
- Source opened: https://thebookofconcord.org/smalcald-articles/part-ii/article-iv/ — [official]
- "This teaching shows forcefully that the Pope is the very Antichrist, who has exalted himself above, and opposed himself against Christ" (§10).
- On continuity without Rome: "the holy Church has been without the Pope for at least more than five hundred years" (§4) — offered as evidence the papacy is not essential to the Church.

### Westminster Confession of Faith, Chapter 25, "Of the Church" (Reformed/Presbyterian)
- **Issuing body:** the Westminster Assembly; adopted by the Church of Scotland General Assembly, 27 August 1647. **Date:** 1646 (drafted) / 1647 (adopted). **Status:** confessional standard, subordinate to Scripture, of Presbyterian and Reformed churches.
- Source opened (original text): https://www.creeds.net/Westminster/c25.htm — [reference/study text, cross-checked against the official comparison below]
- Original §VI: the Pope of Rome "is that Antichrist, that man of sin and son of perdition, that exalteth himself in the Church against Christ, and all that is called God."
- **American revision (1788):** the Presbyterian Synod of New York and Philadelphia deleted this clause outright.
- Source opened: https://opc.org/documents/WCF_orig.html — [official] (Orthodox Presbyterian Church, a current subscriber to the Confession)
- OPC's own side-by-side comparison, describing what was cut: "compared to the total length of the Confession, they are quite minor, involving 145 words out of 12,063" — one of those cuts is the entire Antichrist clause, leaving only "Nor can the pope of Rome, in any sense, be head thereof" with no further explanation.

### «Вероучение евангельских христиан-баптистов» (Statement of Faith of Evangelical Christians-Baptists), Article 10 "Церковь Христова" (Baptist)
- **Issuing body:** 43rd Congress of what is today the Russian Union of Evangelical Christians-Baptists (РС ЕХБ). **Date:** 1985 (published in «Братский Вестник» №2, 1992). **Status:** explicitly non-binding: "принято... в качестве вспомогательного материала для духовного воспитания верующих" (adopted as auxiliary material for believers' spiritual formation, not a creed of fellowship).
- Source opened: https://baptist-volga.ru/verouchenie-baptistov (a regional РС ЕХБ constituent body's page, sourced by its own footer to «сайт Российского Союза евангельских христиан-баптистов») — [official]; the Union's own baptist.org.ru domain was unreachable from this session — see TODO.
- Article 10 defines the Church in purely internal terms and **names no other confession, Catholic, Orthodox, or otherwise**: «Вселенская Церковь Христа состоит из искупленных Кровию Христа людей из всякого колена, языка, народа и племени».
- The confession's only boundary language concerns individual discipline, not other traditions: «От Церкви отлучаются отпавшие от Христа, те, кто связан на земле решением поместной церкви».

### Joint Declaration on the Doctrine of Justification (present-day ecumenical position, Lutheran–Catholic)
- **Issuing body:** the Lutheran World Federation and the Catholic Church (Pontifical Council for Promoting Christian Unity). **Date:** signed 31 October 1999, Augsburg. **Status:** joint doctrinal declaration, formally received by both bodies (the World Methodist Council acceded in 2006, the World Communion of Reformed Churches in 2017).
- Source opened: https://www.christianunity.va/content/unitacristiani/en/dialoghi/sezione-occidentale/luterani/dialogo/documenti-di-dialogo/1999-dichiarazione-congiunta-sulla-dottrina-della-giustificazion/en.html — [official]
- Core reciprocal statement (§41): "The teaching of the Lutheran churches presented in this Declaration does not fall under the condemnations from the Council of Trent."
- And conversely: "The condemnations in the Lutheran Confessions do not apply to the teaching of the Roman Catholic Church presented in this Declaration."
- The Declaration does not withdraw the 16th-century condemnations as historical statements (§42): some "were not simply pointless. They remain for us 'salutary warnings.'"

## Seventh-day Adventists

### "Ecumenical Movement"
- **Issuing body:** General Conference of Seventh-day Adventists (published under "Official Statements → Documents" on the GC's own site). **Date:** 27 June 1985. **Status:** the document is explicit about its own limited status: "The General Conference Executive Committee has never voted an official statement regarding the Seventh-day Adventist relationship to the ecumenical movement as such" — it is a published GC explanatory statement, not a voted resolution.
- Source opened: https://gc.adventist.org/documents/ecumenical-movement/ — [official]
- Self-understanding, combining the ecumenism question with the historicist reading of Revelation 14 and 18: "the Advent Movement represents the divinely appointed instrument for the organized proclamation of the 'eternal gospel,' God's last message," a message "discerned from the prophetic vantage point of Revelation 14 and 18."
- On other churches collectively, using the Bible's Babylon imagery: "In the Advent Movement the accent is first on 'coming out' of Babylonian disunity and confusion and then... 'coming in' to a fellowship of unity."
- On its own role among Christians generally, citing Revelation 12:17: Adventists see the end-time church as "a 'remnant' of Christendom, those keeping the commandments of God and having the faith of Jesus."
- On the World Council of Churches specifically (of which Adventists are not a member): the statement judges that in ecumenism "the banes tend to outweigh the boons," citing a perceived softening of doctrine and of biblical authority as its main objections.

## The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

### "Apostasy," Topics and Questions
- **Issuing body:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (official Gospel Library topic page). **Date:** cited on the page as "(2023)". **Status:** official doctrinal topic summary.
- Source opened: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics/apostasy?lang=eng — [official]
- On the historical churches generally: "After the deaths of the Savior and His Apostles, men corrupted the principles of the gospel and made unauthorized changes in Church organization and priesthood."
- On the outcome for other Christian bodies during this period: "Many churches were established, but they did not have priesthood power to lead people to the true knowledge of God the Father and Jesus Christ."
- On its own claim to be different from a reform of existing churches: "This is 'restored,' not 'reformed,' Christianity," a claim offered to explain why converts "thought they were leaving Christianity" (they did not, in the Church's own framing).

### FAQ, "Are Mormons Christian?"
- **Issuing body:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (official Newsroom/FAQ page). **Date:** current official page (undated). **Status:** official FAQ statement.
- Source opened: https://faq.churchofjesuschrist.org/are-mormons-christian — [official]
- Self-classification relative to other Christians: Latter-day Saints "unequivocally affirm themselves to be Christian, or in other words, followers of Jesus Christ."
- On its relationship to the historic denominational families: "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, however, did not descend from any other faith" — unlike "Catholic, Anglican, Eastern Orthodox or Protestant" bodies, which the same page describes as having "been formed, or reformed, from a traditional line of Churches."
- Restated basis for that distinction: "Latter-day Saints believe that after Jesus Christ and His apostles were killed, the Lord's authority and Church were lost from the earth."

## Jehovah's Witnesses

### "Babylon the Great" (Insight on the Scriptures / Reasoning From the Scriptures)
- **Issuing body:** Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society. **Status:** official reference-work entry, part of the standard JW study library.
- Source opened: https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/1101989211 — [official]
- Definition: Babylon the Great is "the world empire of false religion, embracing all religions whose teachings and practices do not conform to the true worship of Jehovah."
- Scope, explicitly not limited to one group: "it is not limited to one religious group but includes all religions that are in opposition to Jehovah, the true God."
- On churches that call themselves Christian specifically: the same entry asks "Why can religions that profess to be Christian properly be viewed as a part of Babylon the Great," and answers by citing James 4:4 on "friendship with the world."

### "Is Christendom Truly Christ's Domain?" (The Watchtower Announcing Jehovah's Kingdom)
- **Issuing body:** Watch Tower Bible and Tract Society. **Date:** 1 May 1966 (still hosted as current reference material in the Watchtower Online Library). **Status:** official magazine article.
- Source opened: https://wol.jw.org/en/wol/d/r1/lp-e/1966325 — [official]
- Core verdict on "Christendom" (the article's term for the churches historically tracing to the post-Constantine Roman imperial church, Catholic and Protestant alike): "Christendom is not and never has been Christ's domain... but, rather, Christianity's worst enemy and a part of the great worldwide Babylonish religious empire."
- On the doctrinal marker the article treats as proof of this: the Trinity is "one of the outstanding features of Babylon's religion," adopted, in the article's account, only after "Constantine made his decision and came out in favor of the trinitarian teaching."

## Judaism

### Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Laws of Kings and Wars ("Hilkhot Melakhim u-Milkhamot") 11:6–7
- **Author:** Rabbi Moses ben Maimon (Maimonides/Rambam) — an individual legal authority, not a council, but the single most authoritative code in Orthodox halakhic tradition. **Date composed:** c. 1170–1180 CE. **Status:** authoritative halakhic code; this specific passage on Jesus and "the Ishmaelite" (Muhammad) was excised by Christian censors from most printed editions from the 16th century on, and is restored here from an uncensored text.
- Source opened (Touger translation, the same text used by Chabad.org): https://www.sefaria.org/api/texts/Mishneh_Torah,_Kings_and_Wars.11?context=0 — [official]
- On Jesus (11:6): "Can there be a greater stumbling block than Christianity? All the prophets spoke of Mashiach as the redeemer of Israel."
- On both Jesus and Muhammad, in the same passage (11:7): "all the deeds of Jesus of Nazareth and that Ishmaelite who arose after him will only serve to prepare the way for Mashiach's coming."
- This exact passage is cited by name in the footnotes of the 2015 Orthodox rabbinic statement below ("Mishneh Torah, Laws of Kings 11:4, uncensored edition" — edition-specific numbering varies).

### Dabru Emet: A Jewish Statement on Christians and Christianity
- **Issuing body:** an interdenominational group of Jewish scholars (four principal drafters — Peter Ochs, David Novak, Tikva Frymer-Kensky, Michael Signer — eventually joined by some 220+ signatories from every Jewish stream), convened through the Institute for Christian & Jewish Studies' National Jewish Scholars Project. **Date:** published as a full-page advertisement in *The New York Times*, 10 September 2000. **Status:** explicitly a statement by individual scholars — "Speaking only for ourselves" — not a ruling of any Jewish denomination or governing body.
- Source opened: https://www.bc.edu/content/dam/files/research_sites/cjl/texts/cjrelations/resources/documents/jewish/dabru_emet.htm (Boston College's Center for Christian-Jewish Learning, hosting the primary text) — [reference]-hosted [official] statement
- "Jews and Christians worship the same God... Christians also worship the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; creator of heaven and earth."
- On conversion: "Christian worship is not a viable religious choice for Jews," even while "we rejoice that, through Christianity, hundreds of millions of people have entered into relationship with the God of Israel."
- On Christianity's relation to Judaism: "We respect Christianity as a faith that originated within Judaism... We do not see it as an extension of Judaism."
- On Nazism: "Without the long history of Christian anti-Judaism and Christian violence against Jews, Nazi ideology could not have taken hold" — paired immediately with "Nazism itself was not an inevitable outcome of Christianity."

### To Do the Will of Our Father in Heaven: Toward a Partnership between Jews and Christians
- **Issuing body:** an international group of Orthodox rabbis (initially ~25 signatories "who lead communities, institutions and seminaries in Israel, the United States and Europe," growing to 95+); there is no single Orthodox Jewish governing body, so this is a statement by named individual rabbis. **Date:** 3 December 2015 (issued to mark the 50th anniversary of Nostra Aetate). **Status:** statement by individual Orthodox rabbis, not binding on any institution.
- Source opened: https://www.ccjr.us/dialogika-resources/documents-and-statements/jewish/to-do-the-will-of-our-father-in-heaven-toward-a-partnership-between-jews-and-christians (Council of Centers on Jewish-Christian Relations, an academic interfaith archive hosting the primary text) — [reference]-hosted [official] statement
- Central theological claim, citing Maimonides directly: "the emergence of Christianity in human history is neither an accident nor an error, but the willed divine outcome and gift to the nations."
- On present-day partnership, contingent on Vatican II: "we Jews can acknowledge the ongoing constructive validity of Christianity as our partner in world redemption, without any fear that this will be exploited for missionary purposes."
- The statement explicitly credits the Catholic side for the change in relations: it cites Nostra Aetate as having "started the process of reconciliation between our two communities."

## Islam

### The Qur'an on Ahl al-Kitab ("People of the Book")
- **Status:** primary scripture, not a subsequent institutional ruling. Translation quoted: Saheeh International (English), via the official API of the Quran Foundation's Quran.com platform.
- Source opened: `https://api.quran.com/api/v4/quran/translations/20?verse_key=<ref>` — [official] (verses 3:64, 29:46, 2:62, 5:5 individually confirmed by direct API call in this session)
- Qur'an 3:64: "O People of the Scripture, come to a word that is equitable between us and you - that we will not worship except Allah."
- Qur'an 5:5, on Jews and Christians specifically: "the food of those who were given the Scripture is lawful for you and your food is lawful for them."
- Qur'an 2:62, on Jews, Christians and Sabians: "those [among them] who believed in Allah and the Last Day and did righteousness - will have their reward with their Lord."
- Qur'an 29:46: "do not argue with the People of the Scripture except in a way that is best... our God and your God is one."

### The Qur'an on Tahrif (alteration of earlier scripture)
- Source opened: same API, verses 3:78, 2:75, 4:46 — [official]
- Qur'an 3:78: "there is among them a party who alter the Scripture with their tongues so you may think it is from the Scripture, but it is [not]."
- Qur'an 4:46: "Among the Jews are those who distort words from their [proper] places."
- Qur'an 2:75, Kuliev Russian translation (also confirmed via the same API, resource 45): «Среди них есть такие, которые искажают Писание своими языками, чтобы вы приняли за Писание то, что не относится к нему» (3:78).

### A Common Word Between Us and You
- **Issuing body:** a collective open letter by Muslim scholars, clerics and intellectuals, coordinated through the Royal Aal al-Bayt Institute for Islamic Thought, Jordan. **Date:** 13 October 2007 (1428 AH). **Status:** open letter, not a fatwa or doctrinal ruling; addressed to Christian leaders worldwide.
- Source opened: https://www.acommonword.com/the-acw-document/ — [official]
- Opening: "In the Name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful."
- Core thesis: "The Unity of God, the necessity of love for Him, and the necessity of love of the neighbour is thus the common ground between Islam and Christianity."
- Its Qur'anic proof-text, addressed to Christians using the Qur'an's own "People of the Book" language (3:64): "O People of the Scripture! Come to a common word between us and you: that we shall worship none but God."
