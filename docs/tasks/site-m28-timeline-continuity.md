# M28 — Sacred History and Church continuity

## Goal

Make the vertical chronicle express the Orthodox account of continuity from
the Old Covenant to the New Covenant and distinguish ecumenical councils from
the later communities that rejected their definitions.

## Scope

- Merge the duplicated Old Testament and Ancient Israel lines visually, then
  continue the same main trunk as the apostolic Church at Pentecost.
- Explain that the Church fulfils Old Testament sacred history, while the name
  "Christian" first appears later in Antioch.
- Present the Third and Fourth Ecumenical Councils as events of the one Church;
  begin separate lines at the later rejection of their definitions.
- Emphasize 1054 as the Great Schism and Rome's separation without presenting
  it as the first division in Christian history.
- Name 1517 as the conventional beginning of the Protestant Reformation and
  the formation of Protestant traditions.
- Explain the Union of Brest as submission to Roman jurisdiction while the
  Byzantine rite was retained, and record the Orthodox refusal of the union.

## Sources and constraints

Use Azbyka-hosted Scripture, Orthodox historical explanation and Church
documents for the site's doctrinal voice. Use reference history for the dating
of the Church of the East and the Protestant Reformation. Preserve existing
routes and anchors. Do not add dependencies.

## Acceptance

- The biblical and lineage graph shows one main trunk before and after
  Pentecost; rabbinic Judaism leaves it as a side branch.
- The Orthodox trunk is visually stronger than the side branches, whose hues
  remain distinguishable throughout the full chronicle.
- The Nativity row contains the calendar explanation without a separate
  first-year divider.
- The 431 and 451 council rows do not create new Church lines.
- The Church of the East begins at the later 484–486 consolidation rather than
  at the opening of the Council of Ephesus.
- The 1054 row has a distinct major-schism treatment and precise wording.
- The 1517 and 1596 rows carry the revised sourced explanations.
- Timeline tests cover the continuity and emphasis rules.
- `npm run verify` passes and the desktop chronicle is reviewed.
